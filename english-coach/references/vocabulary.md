# Vocabulary Reference — English Coach

> **Principle**: Learn words through *scenes and images*, not through Japanese translations.
> A word is not a translation — it's a *handle* for a concept that exists in the real world.

---

## Vocabulary Learning Pattern

For every new word or phrase, follow this order:

```
1. Scene / Image  →  What situation does this word live in?
2. Example (daily) →  Hear it / say it in an everyday context
3. Example (work)  →  Same word, work context
4. English definition → What does it mean in English terms?
5. 日本語 (only if still unclear)
```

## 1. Essential Phrasal Verbs (General Use)

### come up with
**Image**: You're searching underground and suddenly something *rises up* to the surface — an idea appears.
- "I came up with a solution this morning."
- "We need to come up with a better approach before the meeting."

### figure out
**Image**: A tangle of knots in your hands. You *work through* it until it's straight — you find the answer.
- "I finally figured out why the build was failing."
- "Let me figure out the timeline and get back to you."

### put off
**Image**: You're holding something and you *push* it away to a later shelf. Delaying.
- "I keep putting off the refactor."
- "Don't put off that conversation — it'll only get harder."

### bring up
**Image**: Something was *below the surface* of the conversation — you *lift* it up to make it visible.
- "Can I bring up a concern about the current approach?"
- "She brought up a good point about performance."

### run into
**Image**: You're walking forward and you *collide* with something unexpectedly. Meeting problems or people by surprise.
- "I ran into a weird edge case."
- "I ran into Jake at the conference."

### set up
**Image**: Pieces are scattered. You arrange them into a *standing, ready* configuration.
- "I'll set up the dev environment."
- "Let's set up a call for Thursday."

### look into
**Image**: You're leaning over and *peering* inside something — investigating.
- "I'll look into this bug and get back to you."
- "Can someone look into why the tests are flaky?"

### move on
**Image**: You're at one place and you *step forward* to the next — leaving something behind.
- "Let's move on to the next agenda item."
- "I think we've resolved this — should we move on?"

### end up
**Image**: You started somewhere, traveled a winding path, and *arrived at* an unexpected place.
- "We ended up using Redis instead of Memcached."
- "I started debugging and ended up rewriting the whole function."

## 2. Everyday Idioms (with scene)

### get the hang of
**Image**: You're holding a tool and at first it feels awkward, then your *hand finds the rhythm*. Getting familiar.
- "I'm starting to get the hang of TypeScript."
- "It took me a week to get the hang of the codebase."

### on the fence
**Image**: You're *sitting on a fence* — not in either field, not committed to either side.
- "I'm on the fence about switching to the new framework."
- "Is the team on the fence about this feature?"

### bite the bullet
**Image**: Soldiers bit a bullet during surgery before anesthesia — facing something *painful but necessary*.
- "We need to bite the bullet and do the migration now."
- "I bit the bullet and finally refactored the auth module."

### a ballpark figure
**Image**: Inside a *baseball park* — not precise, just a rough estimate within the range.
- "I don't have exact numbers, but ballpark? About 2 weeks."
- "Can you give me a ballpark figure for the cost?"

### hit the ground running
**Image**: You jump off a moving vehicle and start *running immediately* — no ramp-up time needed.
- "The new engineer hit the ground running — already shipping in week 1."
- "I want to hit the ground running, so can you share the docs before I start?"

### up in the air
**Image**: Something is *floating, not yet landed* — not decided yet.
- "The timeline is still up in the air."
- "The decision is up in the air until we hear from the client."

### drop the ball
**Image**: You were supposed to *catch* (handle) something and you *dropped* it — a mistake of omission.
- "I dropped the ball on that PR review — sorry."
- "We can't drop the ball on the deadline."

## 3. Collocations — make / do / take / have

## 4. Softening Language

> Natives constantly use *hedges* to soften statements and avoid sounding blunt.
> This is *politeness*, not weakness.

### Degree hedges
| Hedge | Feel |
|-------|------|
| kind of / kinda | slightly, informally softened |
| sort of / sorta | slightly, more thoughtful |
| a bit | small amount, British-influenced |
| somewhat | moderate amount, slightly formal |
| a little | small amount, universal |

**Example transformations:**
- "This is wrong." → "This is a bit off."
- "I don't understand." → "I'm kind of confused about this."
- "This is slow." → "This is somewhat slow — worth profiling?"

### Epistemic hedges (softening certainty)
| Phrase | Use case |
|--------|----------|
| I think... | General opinion |
| I believe... | Stronger but still personal |
| It seems like... | Observation, not sure |
| I'm not sure, but... | Lowering certainty |
| This might be... | Weak possibility |
| If I'm not mistaken... | Polite correction of self |

## 5. Words Worth Deep Learning (IT Engineer Context)

These words come up constantly. Learn the *image*, not the translation.

### reluctant
**Image**: Your feet are *stuck to the floor* — you want to move but something holds you back.
- "I'm reluctant to introduce another dependency."

### straightforward
**Image**: A *straight, clear path forward* — no turns, no confusion.
- "The fix is pretty straightforward."

### subtle
**Image**: Something so *light and soft* you almost miss it — barely noticeable.
- "There's a subtle difference between these two approaches."

### trade-off
**Image**: A *scales balance* — gaining something on one side costs something on the other.
- "There's a trade-off between speed and memory usage."

### overhead
**Image**: Extra *weight* sitting *on top* of something — cost or burden added by a process.
- "The abstraction adds too much overhead."

### brittle
**Image**: *Glass* — it works perfectly until one small crack shatters everything.
- "The current tests are brittle — they break whenever we change a UI detail."

### leverage
**Image**: Using a *lever* — getting a big result with a small input by using existing force/tools.
- "We can leverage the existing auth infrastructure."
